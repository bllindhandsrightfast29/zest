The Last Shift at the Cinderwell

A one-shot for Zest · Level 5 · 2014 rules
The clock6 hours Noon now. Eight raiders at dusk. Four people inside who can’t leave.

The Ground

ChandelierWrought iron on a rope-and-pulley over the centre table, cleated at the wall. Cut and re-rig it and it drops. DEX 13 or 2d6 + prone.
Cellar stairSteep, narrow, no rail, 12 ft down onto stone. One way out. Grease it and it’s a box.
Grain chuteTwo-foot square hole in the kitchen floor to the cellar. Cover it with a sack. DEX 12 or drop.
The wellOpen, 3 ft ring, 30 ft deep. Obviously dangerous — so Dissonant Whispers won’t push anyone in. Throw a bedsheet over it and it stops being obvious.
Loose flagstonesThree by the hearth. Lever them up, throw a rug over the gap.
Stable winchRope hoist on a beam over the double doors. Rig it to a tripline and it hauls someone up by the ankle.
Cellar stock11 flasks of lamp oil, a barrel of pitch, 40 ft of rope, a rusted bear trap in the stable.
Front doorOak, opens inward, bar inside. The only way in from the yard.

The Cast

Odessa Vane

Innkeeper, 54

Won't leave. The Cinderwell is the only reason this crossroads is passable in winter, and she knows exactly how many people haven't frozen because of it. Owes 200 gp. Ask her what's in the building and she'll tell you everything — she's your best resource in the prep phase.

Secret: she plans to walk out and give herself up at dusk. She hasn't told anyone.

Pell

Stablehand, 16

Terrified, trying hard not to show it. There's a packed bag in the hayloft and he's ashamed of it. He wants to run.

If you genuinely encourage him — actually say it, don't just roll — he stays and holds the stable door. That delays the two flankers by three rounds. If he runs, they come in behind you on round two.

Halgrim “Kettle” Ash

Crew leader, 40s

Nineteen years a caravan guard captain. Two winters ago he lost all eleven of his crew to a rockslide while he was ahead scouting. He dug for six days and found four. Nobody would hire him after.

He is not evil, he is out of hope. He offers terms before violence and he has never lit a fire himself. He can be talked down, and that is the best ending.

Sisk

Halgrim's second, 30s

Cheerful, quick, and he enjoys the work. Wears a necklace of door-keys from houses he's burned. He threw the lantern at the Ottery place, not Halgrim.

Cannot be talked down and shouldn't be. If Halgrim stands down, Sisk turns on him. That's your climax.

How To Win This

Act 1 — noonAsk questions. Get the full inventory of the building out of Odessa. Find out who bought the debt and why.
Act 2 — the afternoonSix preparations. This is the entire reason this adventure exists. Describe each one. Everything you rig will fire.
Act 3 — duskYour traps go off. You are upstairs with a crossbow, not downstairs with a rapier.
Act 4 — the talkHalgrim. Name his crew. That’s the ending worth having.
