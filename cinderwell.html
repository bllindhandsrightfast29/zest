The Last Shift at the Cinderwell

a one-shot · any class · level 5
The clock6 hours Noon now. Eight riders at dusk. Every hour buys exactly one thing.

The Crossroads

The Cinderwell—Stone below, timber above, a walled yard and a stable. Ninety years of not letting people freeze.
The north road1 hourRutted and pinched between a cut bank and a flooded ditch about a mile out. Anything coming south comes through there.
The crossroads shrine1 hourA stone marker and forty graves in rows, kept better than most churchyards. Someone still cuts the grass.
The Ottery place2 hoursWhat is left of a farm. The barn is a black rectangle. Somebody has been sleeping in the root cellar.
Hensel's drove camp2 hoursTwo hundred head of cattle, four men and a wagon, camped where the grass is still good.

The Building

What's in itA wrought-iron chandelier on a cleated rope. A greased-able cellar stair, twelve feet onto stone. A grain chute. Loose flagstones. A rope winch over the stable doors. Eleven flasks of lamp oil, a barrel of pitch, forty feet of rope, and a rusted bear trap on a nail. An open well, thirty feet deep. One oak front door.

The People

Odessa Vane

Innkeeper, 54

Owns the Cinderwell and owes two hundred gold on it. Grey hair cropped short, forearms like a smith's. Has already done the arithmetic and does not like the answer.

Pell

Stablehand, 16

There is a packed bag in the hayloft and you can see the strap from the door.

Brother-Sergeant Coll

Retired, 60s

Forty years border company. Now he cuts the grass between forty graves and has been happy doing it.

Wrenna Ottery

The one who got out

Somebody has been sleeping in the root cellar of a burned farm. She has a bow and a year of practice.

Hensel

Drover

Two hundred head of cattle, two enormous sons, and a wagon. Moves at first light and says it isn't his road.

Halgrim “Kettle” Ash

Crew leader, 40s

Nineteen years a caravan guard captain. Two winters ago he lost all eleven of his crew and dug for six days.

Sisk

His second, 30s

Cheerful, quick, enjoys the work. Wears a necklace of door-keys from houses he has burned.
