Who He Is

Trait

I learn your name and I use it. Every single time. Especially right before you have to do something hard.

Ideal

Nobody rides the bench. Not the party, not the barkeep, not the goblin who is clearly having a rough week.

Bond

I came up picking locks and picking pockets. The people who pulled me out never asked for a thing back. I’m paying it forward until we’re square, which is never, which is the point.

Flaw

I will absolutely talk to the thing we are supposed to be sneaking past.

The contrast is the characterZest is not a con man pretending to be nice. He is genuinely the warmest person in the room, and he also happens to know exactly how a door works, where the lamp oil is, and which floorboard creaks. He never stops being kind. He also never stops setting up.

How A Turn Works

Every turn you get all three of these. Most new players forget the middle one.

Move30 ft. You can split it — move, act, move again.
ActionOne. Cast a spell, Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Hide, Help, Ready, Search.
Bonus actionOnly if something explicitly says “as a bonus action.” For you: Bardic Inspiration, Healing Word, Telekinetic shove.
ReactionOne per round, and it can happen on someone else’s turn.

Three Rules People Get Wrong

1 — The bonus action spell trap“You can’t cast another spell during the same turn, except for a cantrip with a casting time of 1 action.”

So Healing Word (bonus action) + Vicious Mockery (cantrip) is legal. Healing Word + Sleep is not. It cuts both ways — cast Sleep with your action and you can’t Healing Word that turn either. Plan the order before you commit.
2 — Inspiration can be handed out early and spent late“The creature can wait until after it rolls the d20 before deciding to use the Bardic Inspiration die, but must decide before the DM says whether the roll succeeds or fails.”

This is the single most misplayed rule at most tables. Give the die out proactively — start of combat, to whoever is swinging hardest. They roll a 14, DM pauses, then they decide to add it. You never waste one on a natural 19 again.
3 — You will drop concentrationFaerie Fire is concentration. Take damage and you make a Constitution save, DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. Your CON save is +2. That is bad. Stay 30–60 ft back and it never comes up.

Your Turn, Every Round

  1. Bonus action first. Bardic Inspiration to whoever is about to swing. If it’s spent, Telekinetic shove.
  2. Action. Round one is Faerie Fire or Sleep. Never an attack. After that, Vicious Mockery, the crossbow, or Help.
  3. Movement. Stay back. You have 10 hit points. You are made of paper and good intentions.
  4. Reaction. Nothing at level 1. From level 3 this is Unsettling Words.

Feat — Telekinetic

“As a bonus action, you can try to telekinetically shove one creature you can see within 30 feet of you. The target must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC 8 + proficiency + the ability modifier of the score increased by this feat) or be moved 5 feet toward or away from you.”

You also learn Mage Hand, castable with no verbal or somatic components, and you can make the hand invisible.

Your shove DC is 13Five feet is nothing until it’s five feet onto caltrops. And a silent invisible Mage Hand is how you set, bait, and spring a trap from 30 feet away while standing in plain sight making a friend.
“Anything is a trap if you’re patient enough and you like people.”

The Doctrine

You will never win a damage contest. Don’t enter one.

Every fight you win looks identical: you chose the room, you put something in it before anyone arrived, and then you made them walk to it. The dice you roll are almost never damage dice. They’re the dice that decide whether someone ends up standing where you need them to stand.

You are not the knife. You are the floor.

Combo 2 — The Box

  1. Before anyone knows there’s a fight: caltrops across the doorway or chokepoint. DC 15 is steep, and failing means they stop moving.
  2. Ball bearings on the approach behind it. Prone is advantage for every melee ally you have.
  3. Stand on the far side. When something closes, Telekinetic shove (bonus action) drags it 5 ft back into the mess.
  4. Dissonant Whispers makes it turn and run the whole gauntlet again on its own reaction.
Sequencing mattersPlace the kit before initiative or it costs a full action mid-fight to drop one bag. Ask the DM for a minute in the room. That minute is your class feature.

Combo 3 — Sleep, Then Crit

  1. Sleep. No saving throw. Roll 5d8, drop the lowest-HP enemies.
  2. They are now unconscious.
  3. Any melee attack within 5 ft of an unconscious creature is an automatic critical hit, and it auto-fails STR and DEX saves.
  4. Point at the barbarian. Say nothing else.

Level 5 — The Real Engine

Glyph of Warding

3rd level · 1 hour to cast · 200 gp diamond dust, consumed · until dispelled or triggered

Explosive runes. 20 ft radius sphere, Dex save, 5d8 acid, cold, fire, lightning, or thunder — your choice. +1d8 per slot above 3rd.

Spell glyph. Store a prepared spell of 3rd level or lower. It fires when the glyph triggers.

This is the exploitYou cast the stored spell yesterday, on your own time, out of combat. When the fight starts it goes off for free. You have taken a spell completely out of the action economy. Walk into a room with four glyphs set and you have already acted four times before anyone rolls initiative.
You write the trigger, not the diceThe spell explicitly lets you refine the trigger by physical characteristics, creature type, or alignment, and set a password that suppresses it. You are not making a check. You are writing an if statement and the game engine runs it.
The hard limitIf the surface or object bearing the glyph is moved more than 10 feet from where you cast it, the glyph breaks and ends without triggering. No glyph-on-a-coin bombs. Traps are emplacements. Controlling where the fight happens is the job.

Two Things That Don’t Work

Path to the Grave will not double your glyphThe Grave cleric feature reads “the next time you or an ally hits the cursed creature with an attack.” Explosive runes is a saving throw, not an attack roll. Don’t dip cleric for it.
Snare is not on your list1st level, 8 hour duration, invisible, restrains — and it’s Artificer, Druid, Ranger, Wizard only. Not Bard. It costs a dip, a scroll, or a friend.

Be Curious, Not Judgmental

“Be curious, not judgmental.”Ted Lasso, the darts scene

Ted wins the darts game because for years nobody ever asked him if he could play darts. They looked at him and decided.

That is exactly how loot works. The DM has a room in their head with about forty details in it and they will describe six. The other thirty-four are yours if you ask. Players who find cool stuff aren’t lucky. They ask better questions.

Searching A Room

Don’t say “I search the room.” That gets you one roll and one sentence. Ask these instead.

  • What’s the most valuable-looking thing in here?
  • What looks out of place?
  • What would someone have needed to actually live in this room?
  • Does anything look like it’s been moved recently? Dust lines, drag marks, a clean patch?
  • What’s the light source, and who lit it?
  • Has anything been repaired, and badly?
  • What can I hear? What does it smell like?
  • What’s underneath it? Behind it? On top of it?
  • If I wanted to hide something in this room, where would I put it — can I roll for that?
  • Who made this, and can I tell where they’re from?

Four Questions That Change Everything

1 — “What does my character know about this?”The single best question in the game. It invokes your background, your proficiencies, and your history for free, and it hands the DM an invitation to give you information they were sitting on. Ask it constantly.
2 — “What would it take?”Turns a no into a price. Not “can I climb it” but “what would it take to get up there?” The DM stops being a gate and starts naming a cost you can pay.
3 — “Can I roll X for that?”Propose the check instead of waiting for permission. “Can I roll Investigation to work out who paid for this place?” DMs almost always say yes, because you just did their prep for them.
4 — “How obvious is it that…”Calibration. “How obvious is it that this guy’s lying?” “How obvious is it that I just picked that lock?” Stops you from bluffing into a wall you couldn’t see.

Trapper’s shopping habitEvery room you loot, ask specifically for rope, oil, grease, nails, wire, and anything heavy on a shelf above head height. Nobody else at the table wants it, and it is exactly what your character is made of.

Being The Player Everyone Wants

  1. Hype other people’s rolls out loud. Somebody crits, you make noise. It costs nothing and it changes the whole table’s temperature.
  2. Use the other characters’ names. Not the players’ — the characters’. It pulls everyone into the fiction instantly.
  3. Ask another PC a question in character during downtime. You just handed them the spotlight. Quiet players will remember it for a year.
  4. Don’t solve someone else’s puzzle. If you get it first, ask them what they think. Then agree with them loudly.
  5. Write down every NPC name and use it three sessions later. Nothing on earth makes a DM more generous.
  6. Share the credit. “That only worked because Kira held the door.” Say it in character. Watch what it does.
  7. “Yes, and.” Build on the wild idea instead of listing why it fails. You’re the bard. Momentum is your job.
This is not separate from the buildBardic Inspiration is a mechanic that only exists because your character encouraged someone. You are the one class whose sheet rewards you for making other players feel good. Lean all the way in.

The Bit

Zest is a relentlessly warm, deeply decent man who is also a career criminal with a belt full of traps. He is not faking the warmth to run a con. The warmth is real and the traps are real. That’s the whole joke and the whole character.

He believes in people out loud, at volume, in public, until it’s embarrassing and then a little past that. And he has already worked out which way the door opens.

“I believe in hope. I believe in Believe.”The sign over the door

Prop suggestion: he carries a scrap of yellow cloth with BELIEVE painted on it and tacks it above wherever the party sleeps. Every camp. Every inn. He will fight you about it.

The Goldfish Is A Real Mechanic

At Bard 6 the College of Eloquence gives you Unfailing Inspiration:

“When a creature adds one of your Bardic Inspiration dice to its ability check, attack roll, or saving throw and the roll fails, the creature can keep the Bardic Inspiration die.”

Read that againThey try. They fail. They keep the encouragement anyway. Somebody at Wizards of the Coast wrote the thesis of Ted Lasso into a subclass feature and this is your character. Say “be a goldfish” every single time it triggers. Every time. The table will lose it.

Why Eloquence, Not Lore

This is not just flavor. It’s the mechanically correct call for a trap build, and here’s the proof:

What it can touch
Cutting Words
College of Lore		Attack rolls, ability checks, damage rolls. Explicitly cannot affect saving throws.
Unsettling Words
College of Eloquence		Saving throws. Bonus action, expend one Bardic Inspiration, roll the die, target subtracts it from its next save before the start of your next turn.
Now look at your kitGlyph of Warding — Dex save. Symbol — save. Faerie Fire — Dex save. Dissonant Whispers — Wis save. Vicious Mockery — Wis save. Caltrops, ball bearings, hunting traps — Dex saves.

Almost everything you do forces a saving throw. Lore’s signature feature is the one thing in the game that cannot help with those. Eloquence’s can. And you know the number before you commit, because you roll the die first.
Silver Tongue, also level 3“When you make a Charisma (Persuasion) or Charisma (Deception) check, you can treat a d20 roll of 9 or lower as a 10.” You cannot roll below a 15 on Persuasion. Ever. Zest is incapable of being unconvincing.
Check this with the DMCollege of Eloquence is from Mythic Odysseys of Theros and Tasha’s Cauldron of Everything. Like Whispers, it is not in the 2024 Player’s Handbook. Most tables allow Tasha’s without blinking, but ask. If they say PHB-only, swap to College of Lore — it’s in both books and legal everywhere. You lose the save-debuff, you gain Cutting Words and two extra skills.
